                                                            About MENA Health Data

                                                            An independent, open resource built on public data.

                                                            Why this exists

                                                            Health data for the Middle East and North Africa is scattered across the World Bank, WHO, trial registries and thousands of papers. MENA Health Data brings it into one place and turns it into answers for the people who make decisions: ministries, payers, pharma, providers, investors and public-health researchers.

                                                            Each group has its own workspace with a dashboard, smart tools, a SWOT, the research evidence and data to download.

                                                            Who is behind it

                                                            Dr. Mohammad Al Nawayseh is an Associate Professor of Information Systems and Computing at Ajman University, UAE, with a PhD from Brunel University, UK. His work covers health informatics, mobile health, machine learning and IoT-based healthcare.

                                                            How to use and cite it

                                                            • Everything is free to explore and download.
                                                            • Scores and SWOT flags are screening tools with published rules, not validated models.
                                                            • Study findings are short summaries; read the paper before citing.
                                                            • Suggested citation: Al Nawayseh M. MENA Health Data. 2026.
                                                            • World Bank data is CC BY 4.0; WHO data is reused for non-commercial, informational purposes; check each source's terms before reuse.
                                                            Sources & methods

                                                            World Bank World Development Indicators (CC BY 4.0); WHO Global Health Observatory and WHO Global Health Estimates 2021 (cause-specific death rates, via Our World in Data). "Regional average" is the World Bank Middle East & North Africa aggregate for World Bank indicators and the WHO Eastern Mediterranean region for WHO indicators; WHO Global Health Estimates have no regional average here. Years differ by indicator; workforce and bed figures use each country's latest reported year. IHME Global Burden of Disease data is not included: IHME licenses it free only for non-commercial use, so a commercial platform needs an IHME licence. WHO data is generally published for non-commercial reuse; confirm terms before commercial launch. Clinical-trial counts come from ClinicalTrials.gov (snapshot 10 Nov 2026); the live trial search and live counts call the public ClinicalTrials.gov API from your browser. Country maps use Natural Earth boundaries via the world-atlas package and do not imply any position on disputed borders.
